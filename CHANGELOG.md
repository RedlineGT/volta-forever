# Changes

## Unreleased

### Fixed

- The build scripts (`scripts/build.sh`, `scripts/build-forwarders.sh`, `scripts/package.sh`, `tests/forward/*.sh`, and the tests that look for what they built) built every checkout in one directory, `~/.cache/volta-forever/target`, unless `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` said another. Cargo tells whether what it built is fresh from the times of the files at the paths it read them, and two checkouts build the same units: a second checkout whose files were older than the first's outputs was given them, and its forwarding generator embedded the first checkout's `crates/vf-fwdgen/bf16tc/runtime.c` (reported by a user with two checkouts). Each checkout now has a directory of its own, `~/.cache/volta-forever/target-HASH`, HASH of its path (`scripts/target-dir.sh`); `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` still decides when it is set. The old directory is no longer used and can be removed. `tests/scripts/target-dir.sh` (in the CI and the release check) builds two copies of the tree.

### Added

- After a kernel faults (an illegal address, a misaligned access, a `trap`: errors 700, 716, 719 and the others of their kind), the first call that the layer routes and that returns the error in a context makes the layer say, once and at every log level, which kernels may be the one: those launched in that context since their last synchronisation that succeeded, the last first, each with its grid, block, stream and thread, what the layer made of its image (as the program gave it, PTX retargeted, the PTX of a cubin retargeted, machine code translated and whether its tensor-core products are computed in pairs, compiled alone or with its whole module, from the layer's cache, launched from its second translation), the size and XXH64 of the image given to the driver and the program's image as its load named it; a CUDA graph's launch with the kernel nodes captured in it; and how to know more. A fault that only calls that the layer does not route met (a copy, a memset) is said only when a later call that the layer routes returns the error, and not at all otherwise: the layer asks the driver nothing of its own to find one, neither before a context is destroyed nor at exit; the report at exit lists the faults that were said. A program that calls `exit()` from a signal handler during a call of the driver ends as it does without the layer. Without a fault, the layer makes no call of the driver that the program's calls would not make but two that only read: `cuCtxGetCurrent` after a synchronisation that succeeded and `cuStreamGetCaptureInfo_v2` while a capture is under way (`tests/proxy/driver_calls.py` records the calls). The report at exit (`VF_SUMMARY`) says it too. What a launch keeps for this is written without a lock or a locked instruction into a record of its own thread's last 256 launches: about 25 ns a launch (an empty kernel launched in a loop on a V100 from one CPU: 1.86 µs a launch instead of 1.84, 2026-11-24). `VF_FAULT_TRACE=1` waits for every launch and names the launch (or the graph) whose synchronisation met the fault, alone when no other launch of the context is uncovered, saying that a copy, a memset or a host function before it on its stream is not ruled out, and makes its launch return the error (5.7 to 5.8 µs a launch of an empty kernel). `LIMITS.md`, "After a kernel faults", says what the layer cannot say. The lines never say that a kernel faulted (a copy or a memset, which the layer does not see, may be what failed); 214, 220 and 911 are said to be errors of the hardware, with what ran then.
- `VF_FAULT_REPORT=0` (exactly) turns the report of faults off: nothing is kept of launches and synchronisations, nothing is said, `VF_FAULT_TRACE` has no effect, and the functions routed only for the report go straight to NVIDIA's driver, so that the calls that reach it are those of 0.1.5. `VF_SAFE=1` does not change it.

### Tests

- `tests/proxy/fault.py` (in the CI): what the layer says after a fault, against a stand-in for the driver that shows a fault as NVIDIA's driver does. `tests/kernels/test_fault.c`: its `probe-*` modes recorded what NVIDIA's driver answers to each call after a fault (`crates/vf-proxy/src/fault.rs`), its `rate` mode measures launches per second (`docs/performance.md`).
- `tests/proxy/driver_calls.py` (in the CI and the release check): the calls that reach the driver, recorded between the layer and the stand-ins of `lifecycle.c` and `fault.py` in the cases in which nothing faults, and of `driver_calls.c`, which calls the driver through the pointers of `cuGetProcAddress_v2` (as the CUDA runtime does) and by exported names, with the report of faults and with `VF_FAULT_REPORT=0`: the same but for the two getters the report calls without a fault, each said (any other call fails the test); given 0.1.5's `libcuda.so.1`, the very same with `VF_FAULT_REPORT=0`, on both paths.
- The `fault-*` cases of `tests/gpu/run.sh`: a kernel that writes through a null pointer on purpose, through the layer, run only with `VF_TEST_FAULTS=1` (each adds lines with "Xid" to the kernel's log); by default only the control runs, after which nothing is said.

## 0.1.5 (2026-11-24)

### Fixed

- Building from source on a system newer than CUDA 12.9.2 (Ubuntu 26.04: glibc 2.43, GCC 15): the scripts and tests that compile CUDA sources with the toolkits of `toolkits/` (`scripts/build-bf16-kernels.sh`, `tests/gpu/run.sh`, `tests/gpu/forward-bf16.sh`, `tests/kernels/build-rt-probe.sh`, `tests/proxy/jitlink_objects.py`) failed there with CUDA 12.9.2: nvcc refuses GCC 15, and glibc 2.41 and later declare `sinpi`, `cospi` and `rsqrt` otherwise than CUDA 12's headers ("exception specification is incompatible"; issue #3). They now go through `scripts/nvcc-host.sh`, which compiles a probe with each compiler first and, only when that fails, adds what makes it compile: a `g++-N` of `PATH` that the toolkit accepts (`-ccbin`), and `-isystem scripts/nvcc-host`, whose `bits/mathcalls.h` (ours, nothing of NVIDIA's) hides those declarations of the C library so that CUDA's are the only ones; it says what it adds. Where the probe compiles as it is, nothing is added and the commands are the ones they were. Built so on Ubuntu 26.04 with GCC 14, the bf16 kernels and the translator's test kernels are the same bytes as on Ubuntu 24.04. The toolkit is not edited, and the hand edit of the issue is no longer needed (with it, only `-ccbin` is added). The layer and `vf fetch` compile nothing with the C library's headers (NVRTC has its own); a program's own nvcc of CUDA 12.9, 13.0 or 13.1 fails there as it does without the layer (13.2 and later declare the three functions as glibc does).
- A kernel that the layer refuses, of which a program holds a handle that it did not get from a lookup (cuFFT enumerates the functions of each module it loads through a private table of the driver, and so took, told 8.0, a kernel the layer refuses), is refused with error 218 by every launch function, by a kernel node of a graph when it is made or changed, and by the questions and settings that take a function or a kernel, instead of being launched, trapping and ending the context (Xid 43). The layer asks the driver what a handle is once in each generation of its records, which every load and unload begins (`VF_STATS` counts the questions); `LIMITS.md` says what still traps (a kernel launched or called from another kernel) and when a kernel that runs is refused after it was given (the driver's compiler refusing a function of a deferred library loaded whole, after the kernel was given compiled alone; graph nodes made with it before go on launching it).
- A kernel whose code in its image is a trap and nothing else (how a kernel built for other architectures only is emitted for the others) is given at a lookup and answered about, as NVIDIA's driver does, but every launch function and a kernel node of a graph refuse it with error 218 without calling the driver, and a line names the kernel and its image (`kernel NAME has no code in its image (N bytes, XXH64 X): its body is a trap and nothing else; CALL with it is refused (error 218)`), instead of launching it: its trap ended the context, and the GPU wrote an Xid 43 line. The layer finds such kernels in the PTX that it retargets and in the sm_75 machine code that it translates, not in the inputs of a link (`cuLinkAddData`, nvJitLink), whose kernels are launched as without the layer; code for sm_70 that an image has is loaded as it is and not read. The image is named by its size and digest as the program gave it, or as its file holds it. Strata's CUDA 13 engine 0.1.66 maps the 7.5 it is told back to 7.0 when it detects the layer, and then launched its int8 expert kernels for Volta (`i8h_kernel`), which that build carries only as such stubs, at its first prompt of a few thousand tokens (`native_expert_i8/down: unspecified launch failure`, issue #3); it now stops at that error with no GPU fault, and the Strata guide says which setting avoids it. The proxy's cache has a new format: the first start after the upgrade adapts again.

### Changed

- From `VF_LOG_LEVEL=2`, each image that the layer adapts says how many of its kernels have a body that is a trap and nothing else, and how many an empty body, with the first three names; a kernel with an empty body is not refused (launched, it does nothing, as without the layer). `vf adapt` lists both.
- `VF_PROFILE`: the first synchronisation after a launch that returns an error is said at once, at the default level, with the kernel it followed and its grid, block and shared-memory sizes. A kernel that faults ends the context, and the program learns it at a later call, which names something else.
- From `VF_LOG_LEVEL=2`, the lines about an image that a program loads name it: by the file of a load from a file (`cuModuleLoad`), by its size and XXH64 digest for a load from memory (`cuModuleLoadData`, `cuLibraryLoadData` and the other loads), so that two specializations of one kernel, loaded as two images, can be told apart. The digest is that of the image's bytes, as `xxhsum -H1` gives it, and is taken only for a line that prints it.
- `emulated in NAME` and `kernel NAME cannot run on this GPU` are said once for each kernel: a kernel with emulated instructions is its name and its code (the digest of the image given to the driver, taken only when a kernel of a name said before comes from another load), a refused kernel its name and why it is refused (its code is made to trap). A second kernel of one name (another specialization, which Triton loads as another module), of other code or refused for another reason, was silent, and is now said too, with its image's file, or from `VF_LOG_LEVEL=2` its digest; the same image loaded again is not said again. Refusals of a name said before are said up to `VF_EMULATION_WARNINGS`, then one line says that the next are not; past 4,096 kernels kept, the next are neither said nor counted, and one line says so.
- Documentation: `LIMITS.md` says that the last bits of a Triton FP16 product through the layer can depend on `num_stages` when B is stored N x K (operands copied between the turns of a pipelined loop are gathered, and added in another order than those of a 1-stage kernel; both valid sums), with the cases measured; `docs/performance.md` says how to measure on a V100: settings in turns inside one job, and small kernels timed through a CUDA graph, not one eager launch at a time from Python (figures).
- The tensor-core path of the bf16 cuBLAS variants takes only products with k from 1024 to 16384 (`VF_TC_MIN_K`); a product with a smaller k goes to the skinny path when its rule takes it, else to cuBLAS 12.9 as it is (`docs/emulations.md`, section 11, "The tensor-core path's gate", says why).
- The workspace declares the oldest Rust that builds it, 1.88 (`rust-version`), so that an older toolchain says that it is too old instead of failing on the code; `README.md` says it. 1.88 itself was not run (1.95 was, the oldest toolchain at hand).
- Documentation: `CONTRIBUTING.md` is rewritten for contributors from outside the project (what the project takes, the build, the checks without a GPU, the GPU suites, what a change must show, how to report a bug, a wrong result or a run on another machine, faults and Xid lines, the licence), and `docs/reports/` lists the test reports from other machines, each with its link; `LIMITS.md` says that driver 580.196.07 was reported by users, not run by the project, and that 580.173.02 was not tried; `docs/translator.md` says that the second translation at launch takes a kernel with tensor-core products to level 2 at most.

### Added

- `VF_SUMMARY=FILE`: the report at exit (the kernels with emulated instructions, the second translations, the counts of `VF_STATS`) is also written to `FILE.PID`, and rewritten while the process runs, every `VF_SUMMARY_INTERVAL` seconds (30 by default) when it changed, so that a process killed without exiting (SIGKILL) leaves it. One thread writes it, none without the variable; as the other reports at exit, only the process that loaded the layer.
- `tests/release-check.sh`: the host checks of a release in order, with a table and the exit status 1 on a failure (with `--full`, also the validator over the corpora), and the commands of the GPU part, for the tree and for an installed archive; `VF_RELEASE_GPU_RUN` runs them through a runner of GPU jobs.
- `VF_SAFE=1`, safe mode: the layer keeps its conservative paths. The switches whose results are not bit-identical to the default's are not used, whatever their own variables say (`VF_BF16_TC_GROUPS=1`, `VF_BF16_TC_TILES=1`, `VF_BF16_COLUMN=tree`, `VF_BF16_ROW=tree`, `VF_TRANSLATE=first` and `force`), in `libcuda.so.1`, the nvJitLink adapter, `vf ptxas` and `vf adapt`, nor the pairs of fp16 tensor-core products in translated sm_75 machine code (each product is translated by itself, as with `VF_PAIRS=0` there: a pair adds the halves of k where a product by itself adds the even k, then the odd, and whether a kernel gets pairs depends on its registers and on the block it requires), and one line at level 1 says what was turned off; in the bf16 cuBLAS variants the tensor-core path serves nothing, whatever `VF_CUBLAS_BF16_TC` says, while the skinny path, whose results are 12.9's bits, is not affected. Only the exact value `1` turns it on; it is read once in a process. The capability told, and what gives the default's bits (the `ordered` modes, the pairs of the PTX lowering), are unchanged. The proxy's cache keeps what is made with it and without it apart. The launcher passes it on as it is, `vf env` prints it, `vf doctor` says what it turns off, and `safe_mode` in `scripts/vf` is where the launcher decides it. `INSTALL.md` describes it, `LIMITS.md` ("Safe mode") says what it does not do.

## 0.1.4 (2026-11-23)

### Fixed

- `CUDA_MODULE_LOADING=EAGER`: cuBLAS 12.9, cuBLASLt, cuSPARSE, cuSOLVER and cuDNN 9.10 (its CUDA 12 build) now start through the layer, reached directly or through the forwarding libraries. Under eager loading the CUDA 12 runtime that they carry asks for the module of every fat binary at its start and gave up at the first lookup that failed: one of cuBLAS 12.9's images has ten grouped GEMM kernels for sm_80 that the layer does not translate, so `cublasCreate`, `cublasLtCreate`, `cusparseCreate` and `cusolverDnCreate` failed, and cuDNN 9.10's first operation. As NVIDIA's driver does for an image without code for the GPU, the layer now gives no module (209) under eager loading for an image with a kernel it refuses, and for an image it made from one without code for the V100 when the caller has code for the V100 of its own; their kernels stay given by `cuLibraryGetKernel`. A program of the CUDA 12 runtime whose own fat binary has a refused kernel starts without the kernels of that fat binary under eager loading. `LIMITS.md` says what eager loading costs through the layer.
- A kernel that requires its block size (PTX `.reqntid`; Triton writes it for every kernel) whose usual translation took more registers than that block allows on a V100 was given anyway, and Triton stopped before launching it (`OutOfResources: threads, Required: 512, Hardware limit: 384`; SGLang's MoE router for 128 experts, the gather of 16 warps of the Triton corpus). Such a kernel is now translated within that block's registers, or, when no translation fits, refused for its registers so that the PTX its cubin keeps runs.
- A kernel whose `redux.sync` takes its member mask from a register set to all ones before labels of the line tables and written again later (Triton's code for sm_80) was refused; the mask is now taken from the `mov` that reaches the instruction.
- Triton kernels with `ldmatrix` at a variable of shared memory (`[global_smem]`, in some of its reductions and grouped GEMMs) were refused on both routes; the PTX route now runs them (the machine code of it, `LDSM [RZ]`, stays refused and the PTX kept in the cubin runs instead).
- Strata's CUDA 13 engine on a two-GPU layer split no longer stops on its first request (issue #3): a kernel of a library that the layer compiles lazily, and that only reads lookup tables of its module (initialized data of internal linkage whose address no function stores, passes, compares or gives back), is compiled alone with a copy of them instead of with the whole module, which the driver compiled inside the engine's first graph capture. While such a copy is loaded, the program is not given that table by name (`cuLibraryGetGlobal`, `cuModuleGetGlobal` on a module of the library, and with them `cudaMemcpyToSymbol`, `cudaMemcpyFromSymbol` and `cudaGetSymbolAddress`): error 801 (`CUDA_ERROR_NOT_SUPPORTED`), where NVIDIA's driver gives it, and a line says why. A table asked for before such a kernel is given.
- With `VF_CAPABILITY_ALL=1`, a kernel that a program chooses for the capability it is told, and whose sm_75 machine code is only a stub (it writes no memory, where its code for the capability told does), is refused with a line that says so. PyTorch's attention now fails there instead of computing wrong results with no error.

### Changed

- PTX: an asynchronous copy whose amount register is chosen between nothing and the whole size (Triton's pipelined loads) is one predicated load instead of a byte loop behind a branch: Triton's pipelined fp16 GEMMs at capability 8.0 run 1.3-1.6x faster on a V100, with the same results.
- PTX: two FP16 tensor-core products with f32 accumulators that share their A, both operands loaded by plain `ldmatrix`, are computed together on Volta's tensor cores (half the instructions). The results are the same bits as before: Triton's 1-stage fp16 GEMMs at capability 8.0 run 1.3-1.5x faster on a V100, and up to 10x where the old code spilled. `VF_PAIRS=0` now turns this off as well as the machine-code pairs.
- The documents say what 0.1.4 does: `LIMITS.md` lists its known limits first; `README.md` and `LIMITS.md` describe Triton's machine code as validated on a corpus (3,516 configurations of 20 kernels and 241 cases of vLLM's and SGLang's kernels, on a V100 and by the independent validator), not on every kernel a program may make.
- `VF_CAPABILITY_ALL=1` now tells the value asked only to a CUDA runtime and to the code that uses it. A library that asks for itself (cuDNN, cuFFT, cuSOLVER) is still told what its own code allows; told 8.0, cuDNN's convolutions returned zeros, cuFFT faulted the GPU and cuSOLVER's Cholesky factorisation failed.
- Documentation: Triton 3.8 uses no tensor cores for `tl.dot` on a GPU it is told is 7.5 (it falls back to float32 fma), and in a program with PyTorch it compiles for what PyTorch reports, so `VF_CAPABILITY=8.0` alone leaves it at 7.5, `VF_CAPABILITY_ALL=1` gives it the tensor cores with PyTorch's hazards, and `TRITON_OVERRIDE_ARCH=sm80` fails in Triton 3.8; measured on one V100, a float16 GEMM ran at 0.23 to 3.2 TFLOPS told 7.5 against 26 to 33 told 8.0. `LIMITS.md`, `docs/performance.md`, `INSTALL.md` and `AGENTS.md` say so.

### Added

- Opt-in bf16 cuBLAS variants (`forward/bf16tc/`): with `VF_CUBLAS_BF16_SKINNY=1`, bf16 products of 2 to 16 columns (fewer than 32 when small) with fp32 accumulation are computed by a kernel of the layer, with cuBLAS 12.9's bits, where 12.9 runs its sequential kernel (a rule of the shape, the layout, the alignment and the workspace, measured over 266,195 configurations; elsewhere the call goes to 12.9); with `VF_CUBLAS_BF16_TC=1`, larger bf16 products that are not captured in a CUDA graph also run on Volta's fp16 tensor cores, with exact scaling to fp16, fixed slices of k of 256 and a fallback for the rows and columns that do not convert exactly, differing from 12.9's in the last bits. Both are off by default, and the default forwarding libraries are 0.1.3's bytes. The tensor-core path is for inference: eager PyTorch inference programs (prompts, generation at batch 8 and 32, an embedding encoder) ran 1.3 to 1.7 times as fast with it, and up to 1.9 times under `torch.compile`, but it makes bf16 training slower, the products of the backward pass falling into its fallback (a LoRA training step at 0.56 to 0.81 times its speed without the switch, full training at 0.37 to 0.66 times; `docs/performance.md`, "bf16 products through cuBLAS: the opt-in variants on real programs"). Not there yet: a gate on k, and a safe mode (`VF_SAFE`, which this release does not read) that keeps every call at 12.9's bits whatever the switches say. `cublasGemmEx[_64]`, `cublasGemmStridedBatchedEx[_64]` of one matrix and `cublasLtMatmul` are served; everything else goes to cuBLAS 12.9 as it is. The launcher maps both cuBLAS names to the variants or neither, and refuses to run when they are not all installed; `vf doctor` reports them. The kernels are compiled from the project's source into a checked-in sm_70 fat binary (`scripts/build-bf16-kernels.sh`). `docs/emulations.md` (section 11) states the arithmetic, `LIMITS.md` the limits.

## 0.1.3 (2026-11-23)

### Fixed

- Machine code translated from sm_75 could store wrong values when a store of a register came just before an `ldmatrix` into that register: the translation wrote the load's destinations before the store had read them. Triton's fp32 `tl.dot` kernels gave wrong results, different from one run to the next, at the default settings. Present in 0.1.2 and earlier.
- With `VF_CAPABILITY` at 8.0 or more, PyTorch computed wrong results with no error, and from 9.0 faulted the GPU: it chose kernels that its sm_75 machine code has only as stubs. Callers are now told no later generation than the code they are given (PyTorch, cuDNN, cuFFT, cuSOLVER and the CUDA runtime they use: 7.5), with a line that says so; a program whose code is PTX for the generation asked is told what was asked, as before. `VF_CAPABILITY_ALL=1` restores the old answer.
- With `VF_CAPABILITY` at 8.0 or more, what a caller is told is lowered until the code given for it lowers it no more: given PTX for sm_75, it is told 7.5; given PTX for sm_80, or the PTX that sm_80 cubins retain, at most 8.0; given PTX for sm_90, at most 9.0. A program told 7.5 is given the sm_75 code of every image it loads, no longer sm_80 code (the PTX that sm_80 cubins retain, or the sm_80 PTX of another fat binary of its file).
- With `VF_CAPABILITY` at 8.0 or more, a CUDA runtime is told no later generation than the code of the libraries that use it, also through a library of theirs without GPU code, and at most 7.5 when one of them has code for sm_70. A library without GPU code of its own, beside libraries with GPU code that depend on it, is told at most 7.5 until they are loaded, then what their code allows. This general rule replaces one for cuDNN's graph library by its name; that library now follows the library of engines that the loader actually loaded.
- With `VF_CAPABILITY` at 8.0 or more, a caller whose file was replaced or removed after it was loaded is told at most 7.5.
- The capability told to a main program no longer depends on how it was started (by name through `PATH`, after a change of directory): the program is read through `/proc/self/exe`. Without `/proc`, it is classified by the path it was started by, as in 0.1.2, and its code also limits the CUDA runtime it asks through.
- The capability hooks no longer hold their lock over the loader's lock, which deadlocked a capability query made from a `dl_iterate_phdr` callback. A panic while reading a caller's GPU code sets no limit instead of ending the process.
- In a machine with a V100 and a later card, modules loaded into a context on the later card are given to the driver as the program gave them; a library (`cuLibraryLoadData`) keeps the entries of the other cards beside its code for the V100, and its kernels refused on the V100 are given in its module on the later card; `CU_FUNC_ATTRIBUTE_BINARY_VERSION` is changed to the capability told only for code on the V100.
- A refused or failed kernel lookup (`cuModuleGetFunction`, `cuLibraryGetKernel`) leaves NULL in the handle, as NVIDIA's driver does for a name it does not have, also in deferred libraries. A refusal is still error 218, also for a lookup that waited while its kernel was being refused (it was given the stub).
- Kernels that cannot run on the GPU, in the inputs of the driver's linker (`cuLinkAddData`), are refused at their lookup in the image that `cuLinkComplete` makes, with the reason: also in copies of it, when it is asked for without its size, and when it is put in a fat binary of the program's own or behind a wrapper of the CUDA runtime. Its kernels with emulated instructions are announced. They used to reach the program as code that traps.
- A module or library whose unload the driver refuses, and a link whose destruction it refuses, keep their refused kernels.
- `cuLinkAddData` reads a fat binary within the size given, and passes one of size 0 to the driver as it is; a link made with `CU_JIT_TARGET` for another architecture than the V100's gets its inputs unchanged; a library load with compiler options without values goes to the driver as it is.
- libnvJitLink.so.13: the kernels that trap in a link of which other kernels run are named in the cubin it makes (a `.note.vf.refused` note), and the layer refuses them when the program asks for them, wherever that cubin is loaded (as a cubin, from a file, as an input of the driver's linker, uncompressed in a fat binary or a wrapper). A fat binary in which every kernel traps (PTX, or relocatable sm_75 machine code) is refused, as such PTX given as text was; it used to be linked into a cubin of traps.
- PTX that a cubin retains (`-lineinfo`, `-G`) is compiled again with the contractions that its machine code made: an addition that ptxas did not fuse with a multiplication (`--fmad=false`, a debug build) is not fused by the driver's compiler either. A cubin of which that cannot be told (no line table) is not compiled from its PTX.
- A process made by `fork()` that ends with `exit()` no longer hangs in the reports at exit of the layer or of libnvJitLink.so.13; these reports (`VF_STATS`, `VF_PROFILE`, the summaries) are printed only by the process that loaded the library.
- When a context is destroyed (`cuCtxDestroy`, the last `cuDevicePrimaryCtxRelease`, `cuDevicePrimaryCtxReset`, as on `cudaDeviceReset`), the layer drops what it kept of the modules of that context (adapted images, kernels, originals kept for a second translation, the second translations) instead of keeping them for the life of the process.
- Fixed races in which a module, library, link or context handle that the driver gives again, right after an unload, a link destroy or a context destroy, made the layer drop the records of the new load (its refused kernels were then given as code that traps) or give a kernel compiled for the previous load; the same in deferred libraries (an unload during a lookup, a lookup that waited across an unload and a reload) and in the second translation at launch. A second translation made for a module whose context was destroyed is no longer unloaded by its handle, and the release of a primary context decides whether it was the last before another thread's retain.
- A kernel of a lazily compiled library is given at once while another library restored from the proxy cache is being adapted, and a library restored from the cache is adapted once when several threads ask for different kernels of it at the same time.
- A write to the proxy cache that fails leaves no temporary file; temporary files left more than ten minutes before are removed at the first use of the cache, and one left by an earlier start of a container no longer makes a later write fail.
- An unload of a module waits for a launch from its second translation and for a setting given to it, instead of possibly letting them reach a function of another module.
- `cuModuleLoad` and `cuLibraryLoadFromFile` adapt a file whose path is not valid UTF-8.
- libnvJitLink.so.13: `nvJitLinkAddFile` opens the file by the bytes of its name; a name that is not valid UTF-8 was refused, or another file was linked in its place.
- A fat binary whose compressed entry announces an uncompressed size that no entry has (above 2 GB, or more than LZ4 can make of its bytes) is refused with an error instead of ending the program on a failed allocation; a zstd entry is no longer decoded past the size it announces. The proxy's cache reader no longer reserves more than its files can hold.
- libnvJitLink.so.13: `nvJitLinkCreate` with a null array of options, or a null option, returns `NVJITLINK_ERROR_INVALID_INPUT` instead of crashing; `nvJitLinkAddData` of no bytes or at a null address returns `NVJITLINK_ERROR_INVALID_INPUT` for every input type, as nvJitLink 12.9.2 does.
- `redux.sync` forms that the PTX ISA does not have (a sum of floats, bitwise operations on other types than b32, ...) make their kernel refused instead of being lowered; over a partial warp every lane reduces in the same order; a float `min` or `max` without `.NaN` over a single lane gives the canonical NaN for a NaN, as over several.
- With `VF_PROFILE`, each kernel keeps the counts of its first 64 distinct grid, block and dynamic shared memory sizes: a long-running program no longer makes the profile grow with every new launch shape.
- `vf ptxas`: ends with status 255 on the errors it finds itself, as ptxas; the assembler's messages name the user's input files instead of `/proc/self/fd/N`; `--apply-controls`, `--enable-func-clone-sc` and `--sanitize` take their value, and `-O` no longer takes the argument after it.
- The runfiles views that `vf` makes for a Bazel program are named by a fixed hash and kept under `runfiles-v2`. The program and every process it starts hold a lock on its view, and a view is removed only when nothing holds it, also across machines that share the cache over NFS; none is removed on NFS mounted with local locks or on FUSE. `runfiles-v1`, of earlier releases, is not touched.
- The launcher no longer takes a leftover of an interrupted NCCL download (`libnccl.so.2.vf-part`), or an empty `cuda-12.9.2/lib`, for the CUDA 12.9.2 libraries, which hid a toolkit found next; `vf doctor` says which CUDA 12.9.2 directory it took and why.
- `vf fetch` unpacks each archive and the NCCL runtime in a staging directory and moves them into place with a journal: a fetch that fails or is stopped leaves the previous files rather than a mix, and Ctrl-C, SIGTERM and SIGHUP stop it once the move it is making is done. After a SIGKILL or a failure during a move, the launcher refuses the directory with a message, `vf doctor` says so, and the next `vf fetch` completes the move; it also removes what an interrupted fetch left.
- `vf fetch` says which member an NCCL wheel lacks instead of ending with a Python traceback.
- Run under itself (`vf vf ...`, or after `eval "$(vf env)"`), the launcher no longer adds its `VF_AUDIT_MAP` entries again, and `vf --no-audit` (and `vf env --no-audit`) takes the layer's audit library out of an `LD_AUDIT` that already names it, which the loader otherwise loaded all the same.
- `install.sh` removes the staging directories that killed installers left more than a day before.

### Added

- `VF_CAPABILITY_ALL=1` tells the callers that are held to 7.5 what `VF_CAPABILITY` asks all the same, for a program that does not launch the kernels it would choose for it (the SGLang guide sets it).
